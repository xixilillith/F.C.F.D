# November 17, 2026: First Frame Design - WIP
<!-- fabricate:entry 3362 -->

## November 17, 2026: CAD First Steps

I spent about 8-10 hours today and yesterday designing the first draft frame for my drone.
It took me a while. Something people don't talk about with CAD is the jump from exercises to designing something yourself. Especially something with usually complex geometry.

I eventually managed to get a frame started:
![](https://fabricate.hackclub-assets.com/9f4833e7ee60f3749cd10a8abae5f07ec45540dee8b48b592ad781a5639dcb22/image-png/image.png)
This wasn't the greatest. My plan was to mount the 50x50mm PCB on the 4 pillars, with a hole in the center, where the top piece would rest. This was obviously not a great idea.

I took the time to actually go and look at some other 3D printed drone designs. I eventually stumbled across a yellow one with the top and bottom plates connected via wall (I cannot find the image.)

I took inspiration and strengthened my drone like so, in the process adding arm mounts:
![](https://fabricate.hackclub-assets.com/eed6f134c60a070337757bdf3677c9d0a6d1ac9314d627da132e3c01f6b0779d/image-png/image.png)
One thing I noticed while doing research for this project is that a lot of drones print the arms as part of the body. While this is efficient and quick, if my poor design skills cause something to break, then the whole frame must be reprinted, as opposed to a mounted version where individual pieces can be replaced.

After a long school day, I spent the rest of my night designing arms and choosing motors:
![](https://fabricate.hackclub-assets.com/ae9ba7d947ad7a8cb0abd6e9a3dd99e62aa0a1f301970f9ae39ff4738a2482b8/image-png/image.png)
The arms are simple, spaced with the endpoints at 200mm from the center of the frame, with mounting holes for the 2212 920 KV motors:
![](https://fabricate.hackclub-assets.com/3e3bd5528fd2bdbc8eedf0ed7039199f9f6d1b725d7c7b54e63016d72d8471d7/image-png/image.png)
It was only after designing these I realized the motors screw in from the bottom. Next journal I will fix that for proper mounting, before finally getting to the PCB.
